# 2026-08-19 · Lens 4 scope — what the sample-geometry lens will and will not model

> Not an experiment log. The `docs/02 §9` format assumes a recommendation that
> was actually run; there is no gate output here, only a boundary. This entry
> exists so that later readers — human or agent — stop re-proposing these items
> as unfinished work. Companion to
> [`2026-08-19-lens-7-scope.md`](2026-08-19-lens-7-scope.md).

## Context

Lens 4 (`sample/`, G15–G19) is implemented: NA feasibility, working-distance
headroom, refractive-index mismatch screening, coverslip thickness, and count in
field / overlap. A review on 2026-08-19 rewrote
[`.claude/agents/sample-optics.md`](../../.claude/agents/sample-optics.md) to
match that code — it had been carrying a working-distance formula that
contradicted `free_working_distance_um` — and enumerated the remaining gaps.
Three are closed here as scope decisions, two facts are settled, and the rest
stay open work.

## Facts settled

### 1. Sample-medium refractive index = 1.333

Confirmed as the value lens 4 uses for aqueous samples.
`sample/gate.py::_assumed_inputs` no longer lists it, so falling back to it is
not an assumption and no longer blocks `advances`.

This is a **confirmed default, not a refractometer reading**, and the
distinction is deliberate: 1.333 is a literature constant for water, not a
lab-specific parameter. Contrast `power_at_sample_mw`, which is
instrument-specific and therefore still BLOCKs lens 5 — "we know what water is"
and "we never measured our own laser" are different kinds of gap.

Detail, exclusions, and falsification conditions:
[`kb/expertise/sample-medium-refractive-index.md`](../expertise/sample-medium-refractive-index.md).

### 2. Coverslip thickness = 170 µm, matching every objective's design

KH (2026-08-20): the coverslips in use are the **170 µm** ones, from a product
line that runs 140–180 µm. That matches the design thickness of every objective
on the nosepiece, and sits mid-span of the 40x WI's 0.15–0.19 mm collar range.

**So G18 is not a bottleneck here.** Deviation zero, margin 10.0, ROUTINE.

`SampleSetup.resolved_coverslip_um` still falls back through
`LAB_DEFAULT_COVERSLIP_UM` rather than the objective's design value, even though
the two coincide today. The difference is what the fallback *asserts*: routing
through the lab constant states a fact about the glass, whereas falling back to
design would assert "the coverslip matches whatever this lens wants" and report a
zero deviation for any future objective with a different design thickness.

The value stays `assumed`: a nominal product thickness is not a micrometer
reading of the coverslip on the stage, and `docs/06` records that the real spread
of a nominal coverslip is wider than its stated tolerance. The narrow consequence
is that `status` is **PASS** while `advances` is **False** on the evidence axis
alone — and unlike the refractive index, this one cannot be settled by a
literature value. **One micrometer reading is sufficient and is now the only
thing between an ordinary verdict and `advances: YES`.**

> **Correction, same day.** This entry first recorded 150 µm, then a 140–180 µm
> lot with 150 typical, and concluded that G18 graded MARGINAL and was the
> binding constraint on the lens — recommending #1.5H precision glass. All of
> that rested on the wrong thickness and is withdrawn. The 140–180 µm figure is
> the product line's available thicknesses, not a per-coverslip tolerance.

Detail: [`kb/expertise/coverslip-thickness-in-use.md`](../expertise/coverslip-thickness-in-use.md).

## Decisions

### 3. Wave-optics spherical aberration — will not implement

G17 gates on the `depth × |Δn| ≤ 1.85 µm` screening product and reports the
paraxial focal-shift ratio `n_sample/n_immersion`. It decides **whether a real
aberration calculation is owed**; it is not that calculation, and the ratio is
not a correction factor. No Gibson–Lanni-family model will be added.

The reasoning is not "too hard" but "buys nothing on the axis that matters": a
literature model would be `evidence: assumed` by construction, so it could not
lift any verdict to `advances: True`. Promoting it would take a bench
measurement — focal shift from fluorescent beads at a known depth — and that
measurement is not scheduled. Until then the mismatch is reported as real and
its magnitude as unquantified, and the remedies offered are an index-matched
objective or a shallower focal plane, never a post-hoc correction.

This keeps the risk **named rather than gated**, the same treatment vibration,
stage repeatability, 1064 nm heating, and Faxén drag already get
([`docs/01`](../../docs/01-architecture.md) §7,
[`docs/06`](../../docs/06-pitfalls.md) D5 · D6 · D8).

### 4. Wavelength-dependent and temperature-dependent refractive index — will not implement

`IMMERSION_N` holds one scalar per medium, specified at 589 nm and (for the oil)
23 °C. G17 never receives an imaging wavelength. This will not change, for two
reasons that are worth writing down because the gap looks larger than it is:

- **For the index-matched case it cancels exactly.** The 40x WI is water
  immersion into a water-based medium — the *same substance* on both sides — so
  both indices disperse identically and `Δn` stays 0.000 at every wavelength.
  The case where a wavelength term would matter most is the case where it
  vanishes.
- **For the mismatched case it is a few percent.** Oil's dispersion
  (`vd = 41` → `nF − nC = 0.0126`) minus water's (`vd ≈ 55.7` → `0.0060`) moves
  `Δn` by about 0.007 across 486–656 nm, against a mismatch of 0.185 — under 4%,
  well inside the screening heuristic's own coarseness.

Temperature is **reassigned to lens 8**, which already owns room temperature.
Immersion oil `dn/dT ≈ −3e-4/°C` and room temperature is recorded nowhere
([`kb/expertise/immersion-media-in-use.md`](../expertise/immersion-media-in-use.md)
§3).

### 5. ATPS and other excluded media — ask at experiment time, do not pre-populate

`kb/samples/` will **not** be created ahead of need. ATPS, glycerol/sucrose,
high polymer, non-aqueous, and birefringent media are excluded from the 1.333
default and `sample.gate` BLOCKs on them by design rather than substituting a
value.

The policy is: **ask when that experiment is actually being set up.** Before
then, lens 4 goes no further than confirming *what would need to be known* per
phase — that each phase needs its own refractometer reading, that the interface
itself refracts, and that a field of view crossing the interface cannot be
described by one verdict. It does not chase the numbers in advance, and it does
not treat their absence as an open task.

### 6. G19 count in field — kept, not deleted

Deletion was considered. Rejected, because:

- The separability half (`mean_NN = 0.554·n^(-1/3)` against 3× Rayleigh) is
  computed from bulk concentration, is sound, and **no other lens asks it.**
  Lens 6's G11 is statistical power — too few particles — not mislinking from
  too many.
- `validity/setup.py::resolved_n_particles` uses G19's `expected_count` as lens
  6's particle count whenever one is not supplied explicitly. Deleting G19 would
  silently remove that fallback.
- It is `INFO` kind, excluded from the feasibility grade, so it can never be the
  reason a verdict fails to advance. Keeping it costs nothing.

What *was* fixed rather than deleted: the axial extent. `expected_count` reused
`imaging_depth_um` as the z-extent, which is the whole column — right for
widefield, far too generous for a confocal or spinning-disk section. Because of
the G11 coupling above, that was an **optimistic bias in statistical power**, not
a cosmetic issue. `SampleSetup.observed_slab_um` now takes the extent
explicitly, defaulting to the objective's depth of field when an emission
wavelength is known and to the imaging depth otherwise, with
`axial_extent_source` reported in the metrics so lens 6 can see which it
inherited.

## Lens 4 closed 2026-08-20

Reviewed and closed by the user. Everything below is either **latent** (real, but
not reachable on the current hardware and consumables) or a **named
non-goal**. Nothing here is scheduled work; do not re-open an item as a gap
without a new fact that makes it live.

One thing was considered at the close and declined:

- **Applying Principle 1b to G17's aberration.** G17 currently refuses to
  quantify the mismatch at all, which is correct under Principle 1 but is
  arguably over-refusal under 1b — a Maréchal-style Strehl lower bound from the
  wavefront error would be a *bound* rather than a model. Not pursued (user,
  2026-08-20). Recorded so that G17's silence is understood as a choice made
  with 1b in view, not as an oversight. If it is ever picked up, the test is the
  same as G16c's: does the expression err provably in the safe direction, and
  can its direction be stated?

## Still open (all latent or non-goals)

- ~~Chamber is not modelled~~ — **closed 2026-08-20 as G16b**, but not in the
  shape first proposed. The proposal was a spacer field subtracted from the
  working-distance budget. That was a geometry error: the spacer or gasket
  setting the chamber height forms the walls and is **not in the optical path**
  (either orientation of stand), so it consumes no working distance. The only
  glass in the path is the coverslip facing the objective, and
  `coverslip_actual_um` already carries it — including the thick-dish-bottom
  case, if the user states the thickness.
  What *was* genuinely unmodelled: nothing compared the imaging depth to the
  chamber height, so a request to focus 40 µm into a 20 µm chamber passed every
  gate. `G16b geometry.depth_in_chamber` now catches it — HARD, registered with
  no `requires` so an absent height skips rather than BLOCKs, following G19's
  pattern. `chamber_height_um` uses lens 8's field name so one answer feeds both
  lenses; the 4 ↔ 8 boundary is written down in
  [`docs/01`](../../docs/01-architecture.md) §4.
- ~~Untrapped near-wall drag has no absorption route~~ — **closed 2026-08-20 as
  G16c**, and the same day it was raised. It was first written up as an alarm:
  D8's escape is trap-only, so an untrapped measurement carries the wall bias
  unabsorbed. Two corrections arrived together. **(i)** This lab's measurements
  are mainly *trapped*, so D8's in-situ calibration does apply and the ordinary
  case is not exposed. **(ii)** Principle 1b — the term did not need a model, it
  needed a **bound**. The truncated Faxén factor `9a/(16h)` over-states the drag,
  so "D is low by at most this" is a computation; it reproduces D8's table
  exactly. G16c reports it as INFO when trapped and as `bias` when not.
  `particle_radius_um` and `trapped` are consumed from lenses 7/8 and marked as
  such, the same pattern `emission_nm` already follows — the earlier objection
  that this was jurisdiction sprawl confused *consuming* a scalar with *owning*
  a verdict. No Faxén **correction** is applied, which stays closed
  ([`2026-08-19-lens-7-scope.md`](2026-08-19-lens-7-scope.md) §2).
- **An adjusted correction collar gets no credit for its range — latent.** G18
  compares against a flat `COVERSLIP_TOLERANCE_UM = 5.0` whether or not the
  collar was set. On the lab's 170 µm glass this is harmless: the deviation is
  zero, and the collar clause correctly caps an *unrecorded* collar at 0.80,
  which is the behaviour you want. It would bite on any off-design coverslip —
  an adjusted 40x WI collar would still be judged against ±5 µm despite a
  catalogue span of 0.15–0.19 mm. Fixing it needs a per-objective range in
  `data/objectives.yaml` (e.g. `coverslip_range_um: [150, 190]`) plus a branch
  in `check_coverslip`. Left as a proposal, and **not** a live problem while the
  coverslip matches design.
- **Off-design coverslips are not credited with working distance — latent.**
  `free_working_distance_um` floors the gain with `max(0.0, actual - design)`,
  so a thinner-than-design coverslip buys no extra reach in the model.
  Deliberately conservative for a hard gate and asserted by
  `tests/test_sample.py::test_thinner_than_design_coverslip_does_not_add_working_distance`.
  Moot at 170 µm; revisit only if a real experiment is blocked by it.
- **G17's screening limit is a floating-point knife edge at 10 µm.**
  `abs(1.333 - 1.518)` is `0.18500000000000005`, so `tolerable_depth` is
  `9.999999999999998` and an imaging depth of exactly 10 µm gives margin
  `0.9999999999999998` — **printed as `1.00`, graded `HARD`, does not advance**
  (verified 2026-08-19; 9 µm gives 1.11 and advances). A displayed margin of
  1.00 next to a sub-TIGHT grade reads as a bug and will be reported as one.
  The 1.85 µm limit is a screening heuristic accurate to nowhere near this, so
  the honest fix is a tolerance in the comparison or more printed digits, not a
  re-derivation of the limit. Left open because it is a presentation decision.
- **No multiple-scattering model.** C5's concentration direction stays
  qualitative, indefinitely.
